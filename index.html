Personal memory

My parents designed the house themselves and built it on fifteen acres of woods, hills, and meadow — the last house in Hardin County, or so we said. Beneath the concrete front porch was a cistern that caught and filtered rainwater. The outbuildings held lumber, tools, engine parts, and everything a boy needed to build worlds of his own.

My father cleared the paths with an old Massey Ferguson. He motorized the television antenna on the chimney so it could turn toward whichever station he wanted. Down past the lower meadow, the family kept honeybees.

Most days it was me, the dog, and the woods.

Six inches of powder had come down that afternoon.

One more run before supper. That was the deal.

The Radio Flyer turned beneath me and left me on my back in the snow, looking up into an enormous winter sky.

The kind of quiet where you can hear the cold.

Reconstruction · personal memory · WR-01-01
A pale light stood in the trees
where no light belonged.
It woke.
It watched.Long enough to be sure of me.
Even the snow stopped falling.
And it ran.
No sound. No trail.
Just an empty sky, and a boy on his back in the snow.

I was not terrified.
I simply did not know what I had seen.

For a long time I assumed the night belonged only to me.

Then I started reading.

Context · 1894 Folk-Lore journal · paraphrase

Before anyone measured strange lights, people named them by what they seemed to do.

In Wales they told of corpse candles — a small flame that left a sick person's bed and walked the road to the churchyard, along the exact route the funeral would later take.

A light that hovered over one place marked something buried. A light that kept ahead of a traveler wanted following. Those had names, and rules, and reasons.

But a light that runs is a light with somewhere to be.

And nobody ever gave that one a name.

ERLING STRAND · PROJECT LECTURE

An aircraft approaching head-on can hang motionless in the sky, then seem to leap sideways when it banks. Cold air bends distant light into places it should not be. A child's memory, told for forty years, sharpens in some places and softens in others.

All of that is true. I have checked.

I cannot tell you what I saw that night.

I can only tell you that other people, in other places and other winters, described something that behaved the same way.

It woke.
It watched.
It ran.
