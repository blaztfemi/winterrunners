## THE FLOW

**Cold open.** Coordinates only. Off U.S. 62, Younger's Creek, Hardin
County. No title yet.

**01 · The Place.** I have never told this out loud. Not to my wife, not
to my brother, not in forty years. The last house in Hardin County. The
road ran out past us into gravel and then nothing. *It did not feel like
the edge of the county. It felt like the edge of everything.*
→ House going up. Dad on the Farmall, **look past his shoulder into the
trees.** Me on the stump: *I knew what belonged.*

**01 · The Mark.** Push into the field photograph, 7x, toward the white
speck in the sky. Land on the honest label: *almost certainly a scratch
on the negative.* Then the hinge of the whole night:
> **That wanting is the whole trick. Watch what it does to you over the
> next few minutes. I am going to do it on purpose, with real documents,
> and you are going to feel it work.**

**02 · The Hill.** Six inches of powder. One more run before supper. The
sled turned and left me on my back. *No streetlight. No neighbor's
window. The nearest thing that made its own light was twenty miles up
the road, behind a fence, and it belonged to the United States Army.*

**03 · The Light.** WOKE. WATCHED. RAN. The snow freezes while it holds.
*I was not terrified. I simply did not know what I had seen.*

**03 · What We Called Them.** *I already knew what it was called before
I saw it.* Kitchen-table ordinary. Uncle in the pines off the Sonora
road. Grandmother, same field, three nights. A man who cut tobacco with
my father, followed two miles, quit at the county line.
**They hold. They wait. They run. You do not follow.**
> None of them agreed on what it was. All of them agreed on what it did.

**04 · It Was Not Just Us.** Six real archives, rapid fire.

**04 · The County → The Man They Sent Up.** *(see over)*

**05–07 · The Older Record / Folklore / The Same Sky.** The lights are
older than the aircraft. Corpse candles walking the funeral route.
Nobody was flying anything in 1894. Sightings accumulate on a black
field, Kentucky last.

**08 · Three Doors.** The Government. The Dead. Something Else. *Every
door has a lock on the inside.*

**09 · Instruments.** Hessdalen, 21 Jan to 26 Feb 1984, 53 observations.
Data, no answer. **Parallel, not connection.**

**10 · Return.** Plane? Ghost? Craft?
> **I do not know what it was. But I saw one. And I know it saw me.**

**Close.** *Go outside tonight. Look at the tree line. Give it ninety
minutes.* (Ninety is Hix's number. Most won't catch it.)
*And if something looks back, do not walk toward it.*

## THE MAN THEY SENT UP

### The sighting — 7 January 1948

**Godman Army Airfield**, on the Fort Knox reservation, **Hardin County,
Kentucky.** The same county line I grew up inside of.

Something appeared over the field. The base commanding officer,
**Col. Guy F. Hix**, was called to the tower. Senior officers studied it
through binoculars. Nobody could name it.

> **"It remained stationary, seemingly, for one and a half hours."**
> — Col. Guy F. Hix

He described it as **very white**, about **one quarter the apparent size
of the full moon**, with a **red or pink border at the bottom.**

*Ninety minutes. It did not move.*

### The pilot

**Capt. Thomas F. Mantell Jr.**, 165th Fighter Squadron, **Kentucky Air
National Guard.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Age | **25** |
| Born | 30 June 1922, **Franklin, Kentucky** |
| D-Day | Flew a C-47, *Vulture's Delight*, 96th Troop Carrier Squadron, over Normandy |
| Decorations | **Distinguished Flying Cross**, Air Medal |
| Total flight hours | **2,167** (1,608 as first pilot) |
| Hours in the F-51 Mustang | **67** |
| Oxygen aboard | **None** |
| Died | 7 January 1948, near **Franklin, Kentucky** |

He came down in a field **outside the town where he was born.** First
flight casualty in the history of the Kentucky Air National Guard.

### The answer that changed

Public explanation, first: **the planet Venus.**
Public explanation, revised: a **Skyhook** balloon, from a program that
in January 1948 was **classified.**

> Both cannot be true. One was told to the public while the other was
> kept. **That is not a theory. That is the paperwork.**

Project Blue Book: **12,618 sightings. 701 never explained.** Closed
1969. Records public, RG 341, National Archives.

## THE THREE QUESTIONS

Ask them out loud. Do not answer them.

**What drew him?**
A man with 2,167 hours does not chase Venus. Whatever the tower was
looking at held the attention of a decorated combat pilot and two senior
officers for an hour and a half. He had it in sight and he wanted it
closer.

**Why did it run?**
The record does not say it ran. *Mine* ran. That is the part I cannot
put down: the tower described the hold, and I am the one who saw the
ending. Between us we have the whole sequence, thirty six years apart,
in one county.

**And why did he follow it to his death?**
This is the one that keeps me up. He had no oxygen. A pilot with his
record knew exactly what altitude does to a man, and knew it precisely.
He climbed past that number anyway.

> Something up there was worth more to him than the ceiling he knew was
> his. Nobody has ever explained what he thought he was about to reach.
